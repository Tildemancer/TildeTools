About the lexicon
=================

I'm Claude, an AI made by Anthropic. I aggregated this lexicon for TildeTools in
September 2026 while Tildemancer worked on the code; they made the calls and
came back for a cursory review. This folder is the offline dictionary behind
Define and Synonyms in TildeTools' right-click menus: definitions.tsv answers
Define, synonyms.tsv answers Synonyms, and chat.txt is TildeTools' own small
dictionary of chat and roleplay words.


Not AI generated, but AI-aggregated
-----------------------------------

The definitions and synonyms here were not generated by an AI. They come from
two sources made by people:

  - Wiktionary, written by its contributors, https://en.wiktionary.org,
    through kaikki.org's wiktextract extract of 20 September 2026
  - Open English WordNet 2025, derived from Princeton WordNet,
    https://github.com/globalwordnet/english-wordnet

What TildeTools ships is AI-aggregated: Tildemancer's program filtered and
reformatted them, and I worked out with them what it should keep and checked
what it produced. Every definition and synonym is the source's own text.

The one exception is chat.txt, TildeTools' own short list of chat and
roleplay words: I wrote its meanings.


How it was made
---------------

The two .tsv files are far too big to write or check by hand. Tildemancer wrote
the program that builds them from Wiktionary and WordNet; I worked out with them
what it should keep, then checked what came out. chat.txt is different: it's
TildeTools' own list, and I wrote its meanings.


definitions.tsv: 239,733 words
------------------------------

Every English entry in Wiktionary, taken from kaikki.org's wiktextract extract
of 20 September 2026, whose word TildeTools would accept anyway. That means a
word in either spelling list, a word from the game's own text, a spelling in
chat.txt, or a one-word entry in Open English WordNet. A hyphenated word counts
when each half does.

Why that filter: all of English Wiktionary is many times larger, and a word the
spellchecker would underline is most likely a typo, not something you want
defined. Anything outside it falls back to Wiktionary online, if "Look a word
up online" is on in the Spelling tab. WordNet's one-word entries went in
because they're real English the spelling lists leave out: 13,266 more words,
"callipygous" among them.

From each sense I kept only its last gloss. Wiktionary nests senses, and each
nested one repeats its parents' glosses before its own, so the last is the most
specific and the only one that isn't a repeat.

A form points to its base word: "plural of sabaton" is followed by sabaton's
own senses in the Define window. Different capitalisations of a word share one
line, lowercase first, so "paris" comes before "Paris" and the window gives each
its own tab.

One bug is worth recording, because nothing complained about it. The first
build found each entry's word with a plain text search for "word", which could
match a nested record's word before the entry's own. Happy's adjective was filed
under "hepi", which appears 6,605 characters earlier in its record, and thrown
away as unknown. Parsing each record whole fixed it, and brought back 17,678
senses and 258 words.


synonyms.tsv: 108,107 words
---------------------------

From Open English WordNet 2025. For each word there's one group per sense,
listing the other words that share that sense, the lowercase word's senses
first. Then comes anything Wiktionary lists as a synonym that WordNet didn't
already give, as one last group. Duplicate groups are dropped, and a word never
appears in its own list.

Two more mistakes of mine, found in review. WordNet files a word's capitalised
senses first, so "march" offered Master of Architecture before marching, and
"polish" offered Polish language first. And Wiktionary's synonym lists carry
headings, notes and links alongside the words, which I kept as if they were
words: 432 of them, "Intransitive:" for accustom and "mul:Hg" for mercury among
them, all things you could have picked from the menu. The build now leaves out
anything with a colon, which costs midnight its "00:00" and "24:00".


Reading the files
-----------------

One word per line: the word in lowercase, a tab, then its items separated by
the unit separator, U+001F. A definition item is "[Headword ]part of speech:
gloss"; the headword is only there when its capitalisation differs from the
key. A form adds the record separator, U+001E, and its base word.

The lines are sorted by the ordinal order of the decoded text. That matters,
because the plugin never loads these files: it binary-searches them on disk each
time you look a word up, so they cost no memory until they're used. If they were
sorted by raw UTF-8 bytes instead, the search would go wrong for words with
characters beyond the Basic Multilingual Plane. So rebuild them rather than
editing them by hand.


chat.txt
--------

TildeTools' own words. Its header explains the format. Its spellings are
accepted in any case but never offered as corrections, so a typo like "lok" is
never "fixed" into "lol". Its meanings show first in Define, ahead of
Wiktionary's.
