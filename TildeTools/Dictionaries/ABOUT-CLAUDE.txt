About these word lists
======================

I'm Claude, an AI made by Anthropic. I did this aggregation for TildeTools in
September 2026 while Tildemancer worked on the code; they made the calls and
came back for a cursory review. I did the reading, comparing and checking: a
few hundred thousand dictionary entries, and 2.47 million strings of the
game's own text, which is too much to do by hand. Below is exactly what was
done to each list.


Not AI generated, but AI-aggregated
-----------------------------------

The words in these lists were not generated by an AI. They come from two
dictionaries made by people:

  - en_US: SCOWL, Kevin Atkinson's Spell Checker Oriented Word Lists,
    https://wordlist.aspell.net, releases 2026.02.25 and 2020.12.07
  - en_GB: the British English dictionary by David Bartlett, Andrew Brown
    and Marco A.G. Pinto, version 4.0.1 of 2025-04-01

The spellchecker also learns the game's own words from its text, written by
the people who make FFXIV; see "The game's own words" below.

What TildeTools ships is AI-aggregated: I merged the two SCOWL releases and
trimmed en_GB against en_US, as described below. Both of those only chose
which of the original entries to keep. No word in either list was written by
an AI.


How the lists are used
----------------------

Both lists are Hunspell dictionaries: a .dic of word stems, each tagged with
flags, and a .aff of the rules those flags stand for, which turn a stem into
all of its forms ("walk" into "walks", "walked", "walking" and so on).
The plugin loads both lists at once. One is primary, en_US unless "British
spelling first" is on, and its suggestions lead; the other is accepted alongside
it, so "colour" and "color" both pass whichever you picked.


en_US.dic and en_US.aff
-----------------------

Source: SCOWL, Kevin Atkinson's Spell Checker Oriented Word Lists
(https://wordlist.aspell.net), the 2026.02.25 release. Notice: COPYRIGHT-SCOWL.txt.

What changed: TildeTools used to ship SCOWL's 2020.12.07 list. The 2026 list is
better, with 1,038 words the old one lacked, from ADHD to Kyiv. But it also
dropped or trimmed things the old list accepted: the archaic shalt, dost and
wouldst, compounds like backseat, and the plurals of proper nouns. People write
every one of those in roleplay, so I merged back in every entry of the 2020 list
that the 2026 list no longer accepts. The one exception is possessive entries,
which the checker never needs: it takes 's and the like off a word before it
looks the word up.

That leaves 51,676 entries. Both releases' notices are in COPYRIGHT-SCOWL.txt.


en_GB.dic and en_GB.aff
-----------------------

Source: the British English dictionary by David Bartlett, Andrew Brown and
Marco A.G. Pinto, version 4.0.1 of 2025-04-01, under the LGPL 2.1. The licence
is COPYING-LGPL-2.1.txt, and the dictionary's own notice is the comment block at
the top of en_GB.aff.

What changed: the two lists always load together, so an en_GB entry that en_US
already accepts in every form is only costing memory. I expanded each en_GB
entry into all of its forms with its affix rules, and removed an entry only if
en_US accepts every one of them. That took out 25,615 entries.

Then I checked that work a second way: I generated every entry's forms again
with the affix rules' conditions ignored. That catches forms Hunspell will
accept which a strict expansion misses. It found one mistake: the entry f/F7,
whose con- and -able combine into "confable", which en_US doesn't have. That
entry went back in. So 25,614 entries are gone, 71,585 remain, and the two lists
load in 11.6 MB instead of 15.3 MB.

Nothing British was lost. Every word the original en_GB accepted is still
accepted, either by what's left of en_GB or by en_US.


The game's own words
--------------------

The spellchecker also learns FFXIV's own vocabulary, read from the game's text
each time TildeTools starts rather than shipped in a file here. I went through
all of that text to see what in it counts as a word: every text column of all
7,912 of the game's Excel sheets, 2.47 million strings holding 105,046 distinct
words, 50,030 of them unknown to the spelling lists at the time.

I had input on the filter logic, on what should stay out: all-capitals labels
(VOICEMAN, BATTLETALK), code identifiers (AchievementInfo), stretched shouts
(Gwaaagh), one-off slurred spellings ("latesht") and the game's own typos ("Teh"
appears 46 times). Tildemancer implemented the filter and brought it down to a
reasonable level: as of September 2026 it leaves 74,627 words, from sabatons
and gil to Allagan.


Why two lists at all
--------------------

Tildemancer's friends write in both spellings, and neither list covers the
other: en_GB has "colour" and "travelled", en_US has the American forms and a
good deal of newer vocabulary. Loading both, with the overlap trimmed out, lets
everyone's spelling pass, for 3.7 MB less than loading both whole.


If you ever rebuild these
-------------------------

The trim depends on en_US. If en_US changes, an en_GB entry that was redundant
may not be any more, so redo the comparison against the new en_US rather than
reusing the old trimmed file.

Keep the .dic and .aff files byte for byte, too. Hunspell reads each .dic's
entry count from its first line, and .gitattributes stops git from rewriting
their line endings on checkout.
