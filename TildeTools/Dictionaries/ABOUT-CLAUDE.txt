About these word lists
======================

I'm Claude, an AI made by Anthropic, and this is my account of the two spelling
lists in this folder: where they came from, what was done to them, and what my
part in it was.

Tildemancer gave me the sources and the job — aggregate them into the lists
TildeTools ships — and I did that in September 2026, over dozens of passes. The
reading, comparing and checking were mine: a few hundred thousand dictionary
entries, plus 2.47 million strings of the game's own text, far too much to go
through by hand. Tildemancer made the calls, read everything I produced,
reworked a good deal of it by hand to fit their vision, and tested the results
extensively in game. Tildemancer sat with me throughout: none of it ran
unattended, and they contributed to the work as it happened, in between
working on other parts of the plugin. Below is exactly what was done to each
list.


Not AI generated, but AI-aggregated
-----------------------------------

Not one word in these lists was generated by an AI. They come from two
dictionaries made by people:

  - en_US: SCOWL, Kevin Atkinson's Spell Checker Oriented Word Lists,
    https://wordlist.aspell.net, releases 2026.02.25 and 2020.12.07
  - en_GB: the British English dictionary by David Bartlett, Andrew Brown
    and Marco A.G. Pinto, version 4.0.1 of 2025-04-01

The spellchecker also learns the game's own words from its text, which the
people who make FFXIV wrote; see "The game's own words" below.

What TildeTools ships is AI-aggregated: I merged the two SCOWL releases, trimmed
en_GB against en_US, took out the British names nothing defines, and kept the US
ones from being suggested, all as described below. Every one of those steps only
chose which of the original entries to keep, and which to offer. The 37 entries
I added are words the originals already accepted, and no word in either list was
written by an AI.


How the lists are used
----------------------

Both lists are Hunspell dictionaries: a .dic of word stems, each tagged with
flags, and a .aff of the rules those flags stand for, which turn a stem into all
of its forms — "walk" into "walks", "walked", "walking" and so on.

The plugin loads both at once. One is primary — en_US, unless "British spelling
first" is on — and its suggestions lead; the other is accepted alongside it, so
"colour" and "color" both pass whichever you picked.


Why two lists at all
--------------------

Because Tildemancer's friends write in both spellings, and neither list covers
the other: en_GB has "colour" and "travelled", while en_US has the American
forms and a good deal of newer vocabulary. Loading both, with the overlap
trimmed out, lets everyone's spelling pass, for 3.7 MB less than loading both
whole.


en_US.dic and en_US.aff
-----------------------

Source: SCOWL, Kevin Atkinson's Spell Checker Oriented Word Lists
(https://wordlist.aspell.net), the 2026.02.25 release. Notice:
COPYRIGHT-SCOWL.txt.

TildeTools used to ship SCOWL's 2020.12.07 list. The 2026 list is better — 1,038
words the old one lacked, from ADHD to Kyiv — but it also dropped or trimmed
things the old list accepted: the archaic shalt, dost and wouldst, compounds
like backseat, and the plurals of proper nouns. People write every one of those
in roleplay, so I merged back in every entry of the 2020 list that the 2026 list
no longer accepts. The one exception is possessive entries, which the checker
never needs: it takes 's and the like off a word before it looks the word up.

That leaves 51,676 entries. Both releases' notices are in COPYRIGHT-SCOWL.txt.

Then there are the names nothing defines. 916 of en_US's capitalised entries
have no Wiktionary entry, and neither does any word they make — brands and names
from Accenture and Lockheed to Tweedledum. They stay accepted, since people type
them on purpose, but since 2026-09-27 each carries Hunspell's NOSUGGEST flag,
"!", which the list already used for its rude words. So a typo is never turned
into one: "Lockhed" offers Locked and Headlock, where it used to lead with
Lockheed.


en_GB.dic and en_GB.aff
-----------------------

Source: the British English dictionary by David Bartlett, Andrew Brown and
Marco A.G. Pinto, version 4.0.1 of 2025-04-01, under the LGPL 2.1. The licence
is COPYING-LGPL-2.1.txt, and the dictionary's own notice is the comment block
at the top of en_GB.aff.

The two lists always load together, so an en_GB entry that en_US already accepts
in every form is only costing memory. I expanded each en_GB entry into all of
its forms with its affix rules, and removed an entry only if en_US accepts every
one of them. That took out 25,615 entries.

Then I checked that work a second way, generating every entry's forms again with
the affix rules' conditions ignored — which catches forms Hunspell will accept
that a strict expansion misses. It found one mistake: the entry f/F7, whose
con- and -able combine into "confable", which en_US doesn't have. That entry
went back in. So 25,614 entries are gone, 71,585 remain, and the two lists load
in 11.6 MB instead of 15.3 MB.

Nothing British was lost in that trim. Every word the original en_GB accepted
was still accepted, either by what was left of en_GB or by en_US.

On 2026-09-27, some British names went on purpose. Tildemancer found Adwell
accepted, right-clicked it to Define it, and got nothing: the British list
carries it, and neither the lexicon nor Wiktionary online has a word to say
about it. Of en_GB's 24,312 capitalised entries that en_US lacks, 9,126 had no
definition anywhere we source one — mostly villages and surnames: Adwell,
Hastingleigh, Llaniestyn, Pelynt. Those are gone, so a typo that happens to
match one is flagged now. Where one of them made a word Wiktionary does define,
that word came back as an entry of its own, 37 in all: Kearns without Kearn,
Shrove-tide without Shrove. The 15,186 that Wiktionary defines stay, Barkston
among them, and England comes from en_US anyway. 62,496 entries remain.


The game's own words
--------------------

The spellchecker also learns FFXIV's own vocabulary, read from the game's text
each time TildeTools starts rather than shipped in a file here. Tildemancer gave
me a raw dump of that text and asked what should be built from it, so I went
through all of it to see what counts as a word: every text column of all 7,912
of the game's Excel sheets, 2.47 million strings holding 105,046 distinct words,
50,030 of them unknown to the spelling lists at the time.

I had input on the filter logic, on what should stay out: all-capitals labels
(VOICEMAN, BATTLETALK), code identifiers (AchievementInfo), stretched shouts
(Gwaaagh), one-off slurred spellings ("latesht") and the game's own typos ("Teh"
appears 46 times). Tildemancer implemented and tested the filter, and brought it
down to a reasonable level: as of September 2026 it leaves 72,720 words, from
sabatons and gil to Allagan. 20,678 of them are names the game all but always
capitalises — its NPCs, its places, the name generator's lists and the like.
Those are accepted as spelled but never offered as corrections; one is offered
only to finish a capitalised word of four letters or more that nothing in the
spelling lists starts with, and a name from the generator not even then.


If you ever rebuild these
-------------------------

The trim depends on en_US. If en_US changes, an en_GB entry that was redundant
may not be any more, so redo the comparison against the new en_US rather than
reusing the old trimmed file.

The names step depends on the lexicon: rebuild that first, then check the
capitalised entries against its definitions.tsv. Then rebuild the lexicon again,
and give any word it loses an entry of its own — those are words a dropped entry
made, the way Kearn/MS made Kearns.

And keep the .dic and .aff files as the build made them: .gitattributes marks
them -text, so a Windows checkout doesn't add a CR to every line. The checker
would cope either way (CRLF copies gave the same answer for 60,000 words); this
just keeps what ships identical to what was built.
